How it works

From suspicious post to explained result in three steps.

No dashboards to learn, no accounts to create. Paste, upload, decide.

01

Submit the content

Paste a post, headline or comment, or drop an image or video file. Filipino, Taglish and English are all supported.

02

Signals are scored

Lexical sensationalism, attribution integrity, emotional load, formatting, verifiability and file metadata are each measured separately.

03

Read the reasoning

You get a verdict, a 0–100 risk score, the flagged passages, and a short list of what to check next.

Coverage

Three ways content spreads. Three ways to check it.

Text

Fake news & clickbait

Headlines, articles, posts and group-chat forwards are screened for sensational framing, unnamed sources, hedging words like daw and umano, and missing checkable detail.

Image

Manipulated photos

Container bytes, EXIF and C2PA provenance, editing-software signatures, generator fingerprints in filenames, and geometry defaults typical of diffusion models.

Video

Face-swap deepfakes

Clip duration, resolution, bitrate profile, container metadata and audio-track presence, plus the filename fingerprints that synthetic-media tools leave behind.

Why local matters

Western detectors read Taglish like noise.

Code-switching, regional particles and slang break tokenisers trained on English newsrooms. The result is exactly the gap this study targets: content that spreads fastest is the content the models understand least.

  • Language support covers Filipino and English, including everyday Taglish.
  • Hedging markers lifted from local news culture — daw, umano, kuno — are scored directly.
  • Confidence is presented with the reasoning, so users learn to spot the pattern themselves.
