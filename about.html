Development of an AI-Based System for Fake News and Deepfake Detection in Filipino Digital Content.

The full rationale, research questions, scope and acknowledged limits behind the Totoo AI prototype.

ResearchersLedesma, Trishia G.
Fellesco, Justin Ace P.
Tampe, Rhian Jay
GroupGroup 10
FocusFilipino · Taglish · English digital content

Prototype disclosure. This website is the functional interface for the system proposed in the study. The detection layer implements the study's signal families as transparent, on-device heuristics. It is not a trained DistilBERT, CNN-LSTM or multimodal transformer — those are the study's proposed production models. Read every result as a demonstration of the workflow.

1. Background of the study

Artificial intelligence and digital communication have changed how information is made, shared and used worldwide. Social media made sharing easier — and made false information travel faster. One emerging form is the deepfake: video or audio generated with AI so that real people appear to do or say things they never did. In many countries, fake videos and fake news have moved from a political nuisance into a national-security concern because they can break trust, distort elections, and destabilise communities.

In the Philippines — a country with very high social media usage — the effects are serious. Most conversation happens in Tagalog, regional languages, or Taglish, the mix of Tagalog and English. Verification tools built in the West often fail here because of local expressions, phrasing, and code-switching. As deepfake tools get cheaper, bad actors use them against individuals, journalists, and government institutions. Fact-checking has not kept pace with how fast manipulated content spreads on Facebook, TikTok and X.

Most published detection research is trained on English or European data. There is a clear gap in applying advanced models to the complicated, mixed language used online in the Philippines — leaving Filipinos exposed to increasingly sophisticated deception. This study sets out to design, build and evaluate an AI system tailored to that environment, combining language models and computer vision to identify fake videos and false messages quickly and accurately, so that fact-checkers can protect the integrity of public conversation.

2. Statement of the problem

The growth of AI-generated deepfakes and false information threatens online safety and political discourse in the Philippines. Despite heavy media use, local fact-checking and deepfake detection remain inadequate: most systems are built on foreign languages and datasets, and they break down on Taglish and regional languages. Bad actors exploit this weakness against individuals, reporters and government groups, leaving fact-checkers without the tools to stop fast-spreading content on Facebook, TikTok and X.

Specifically, the study answers these questions:

  1. What are the technical and linguistic features of the deepfakes and false news that are common on Philippine social media platforms like Facebook, TikTok and X?
  2. How can natural language processing models be trained to correctly detect false information written in Tagalog, Taglish and selected local languages?
  3. How well does combining computer vision with locally adapted language models work in detecting manipulated videos, compared with Western-built models?
  4. How well does the developed system perform in terms of accuracy, precision, recall and processing speed when tested on a collection of Philippine online content?
  5. How can the system help fact-checkers and information regulators in the Philippines do their work better and faster?

3. Objectives

General objective

To develop an AI-based system that can detect fake news and deepfake content in Filipino digital media, helping identify misleading information and improve the reliability of content shared on social media platforms.

Specific objectives

  1. Identify the common features of fake news and deepfake content shared on social media platforms in the Philippines.
  2. Develop an AI-based system that can detect false information written in Filipino, Taglish and selected local languages.
  3. Integrate natural language processing and computer vision to analyse text, images and videos.
  4. Evaluate the system's performance in terms of accuracy, precision, recall and processing speed.
  5. Determine how the system can help users and fact-checkers identify misleading information more efficiently.

4. Scope

The system focuses on identifying and detecting fake news and deepfake content in Filipino digital media, analysing text, images and videos shared through digital platforms. Specifically:

  • Fake news detection — Filipino and English text content, including news articles, headlines and social media posts, screened for misleading, fabricated or potentially false information.
  • Deepfake detection — uploaded images and videos examined for signs of AI generation or digital manipulation, such as unnatural facial movement and visual inconsistency.
  • Filipino language support — primarily Filipino and English, including common expressions used in Philippine digital communication.
  • AI-based analysis — detection results generated through AI and machine-learning techniques.
  • User-friendly interface — a simple interface where users upload images, videos or text and receive a result indicating whether content is likely authentic, fake, or potentially manipulated.
  • Detection results — a classification result and a confidence score indicating the likelihood that submitted content contains misinformation or deepfake manipulation.

5. Limitations

The study acknowledges conditions that affect accuracy and performance:

  1. Limited dataset availability — accuracy depends on the quality, quantity and diversity of training and testing data; limited Filipino fake-news and deepfake datasets constrain performance.
  2. Language limitations — regional languages, dialects, slang, mixed-language expressions and unfamiliar terms may be misread.
  3. Detection accuracy — sophisticated manipulation may go undetected, while legitimate content may occasionally be flagged.
  4. Limited media formats — only selected text, image and video formats are supported; live streams, audio recordings and real-time posts are out of scope.
  5. Dependence on uploaded content — the system analyses only what users submit; it does not monitor platforms automatically.
  6. Evolving AI technology — newly developed manipulation methods may not be recognised without regular dataset and model updates.
  7. Internet and technical requirements — some features may need connectivity, sufficient processing power and compatible devices.
  8. Not a substitute for fact-checking — the system assists; it does not guarantee truthfulness. Users must still verify through credible sources and fact-checking organisations.

Status. The study positions the system as a supplementary verification tool, not a replacement for professional fact-checking. Totoo AI adopts the same stance throughout its interface.

6. Significance of the study

  • Social media users — helps them identify fake news and deepfake content before believing or sharing it.
  • Students — raises awareness of AI-generated content and encourages checking reliability before academic use.
  • Teachers — serves as a learning resource on misinformation, deepfakes and responsible digital media use.
  • Fact-checkers and journalists — provides an additional triage tool for spotting suspicious digital content more efficiently.
  • Future researchers and developers — offers a reference for improving AI detection systems designed for Filipino languages and content.

7. How this prototype maps to the objectives

Objective Implemented as
Multi-modal analysis Text, image and video tabs share one result schema and one risk scale.
Filipino language support Lexicons and hedging rules written for Filipino, Taglish and English.
Explainable confidence A 0–100 risk score with per-signal values and flagged passages.
Evaluation Local scan history, verdict distribution, and published benchmarks side by side.
Fact-checker workflow Every result links to VERA Files, Tsek.ph, Rappler, PCIJ and Google Fact Check.

8. Technology stack

The prototype is deliberately dependency-free: HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript, with no build step and no server-side processing. Text and media are analysed in the browser, and scan history is stored in localStorage. This keeps the demonstration reproducible on any laptop during a defense, and keeps submitted content private. The TotooEngine module exposes one function per modality, so a hosted transformer model or a CNN-based deepfake detector can replace the heuristic layer without touching the interface.
